Section 1

It was a very warm Thursday evening, expected seeing as it had been hotter earlier. I was still getting used to the festering heat in this part of the world but the heat would have to wait. Here I was, standing in the small but very clean back kitchen of my Granduncle’s house, knife in one hand and blood in the other.

I had just committed murder.

In all this, I was not scared. Maybe because just crouched to my left was my senior brother, blood on both his hands and gesturing to me to pass him the knife to complete the massacre. He was pretty eager because he wanted to get done with it, clean up, refrigerate, and get some sleep. He’s usually not impatient but today he was.

Turning to face him, I handed him the knife, and crouched beside him, inhaling the smell of his deodorant, the warmth of our breaths adding to the already humid air that hung heavily above and beneath us.

We had just killed a chicken.

My brother and I share very deep similarities, mostly because I adore him very much plus his unconditional love for me, so we easily pick up whatever behaviors the other is exhibiting at any point in time; a shared existence. And one thing we share very much is being prepared before we engaged in anything novel or unusual. Contingencies always had to be made.

But when our host’s wife had shown us the very big and healthy cock she had bought at Ogige market and asked us that we killed and cut it up, our honest response that we hadn’t done anything remotely related to taking life had been met with snide remarks and heaps of mockery, another thing we shared kicked in; proving people wrong.

So we sat and ended the life of that chicken, almost enjoying it while the hot water boiled. Plucked every feather till its skin was as smooth as glass and sliced it up with almost surgical precision, placing chunks into plastic bags for storage in the freezer.

This was 10 years ago. Back then, the heat, the blood, and the sweat felt like a one-off ordeal. But looking back now, that small kitchen taught me something bigger: life itself is like that, messy, humid, sometimes uncomfortable, yet the only way forward is to get your hands dirty and learn as you go. I went through life without properly anchoring this experience in my psyche and almost certainly reverted to ensuring preparedness before doing things but a rude awakening a decade later reminded me of the importance of doing things without waiting to know how every cog in the wheel moves. I saw this firsthand with my mentor, with how he picks up things and sets to do them whilst still being a complete novice. In his words “life is like a video game, every challenge is like a level, and beating that challenge saves the checkpoint.”
